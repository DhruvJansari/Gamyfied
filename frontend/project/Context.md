StandByDev

Tagline: Where developers establish their standing.

StandByDev is a developer reputation, ranking, competition, and discovery platform built around one idea:

The work you do as a developer should contribute to your standing as a developer.

Today, developers build projects, contribute to repositories, maintain portfolios, participate in communities, learn new skills, and solve problems — but much of that work remains scattered across different platforms and is difficult to turn into a meaningful, continuously evolving developer identity.

StandByDev brings these activities together into a system where developers can build, contribute, prove their work, gain reputation, establish a rank, compete, grow, and connect with other developers.

1. Developer Identity

Every developer has a persistent identity on StandByDev.

The profile is not intended to be another traditional resume.

Instead, it represents the developer through things such as:

Skills and technologies
Projects and work
Professional identities
Contributions
Reputation
Rank
Standing within the developer community
Participation and activity
Clan membership and performance
Community presence

Each developer also receives a permanent developer ID.

The ID stays associated with the developer even if they change their username or profile details.

The goal is to create a developer identity that evolves over time rather than becoming a static profile that rarely changes.

2. Developer Reputation

The central concept of StandByDev is reputation.

A developer's reputation should come from the things they actually do and contribute rather than simply what they claim about themselves.

Developers can build reputation through different forms of activity and evidence, including:

Projects they build
Verified professional identities
Contributions
Participation
Community activity
Popularity
Clan performance
Competitions
Other meaningful activity on the platform

Reputation is therefore intended to represent an evolving picture of a developer's participation and standing.

3. Proof and Verification

A major part of the platform is distinguishing between claims and evidence.

Developers can provide evidence of their work and professional presence.

StandByDev can use verification systems to evaluate submitted evidence and determine how much credibility it should contribute to a developer's reputation.

Examples include verification of:

Websites and projects
GitHub activity
LeetCode activity
LinkedIn identity
Other supported professional evidence

The purpose is not to make every developer prove everything.

The purpose is to make reputation increasingly based on observable evidence rather than unsupported claims.

Unverified information can still exist on a profile, but verified evidence carries greater weight.

4. Projects and Portfolios

Projects are an important part of a developer's standing.

Developers can showcase projects they have built, including links such as:

Live websites
GitHub repositories
Other relevant project evidence

Projects can contribute to reputation depending on the available evidence and verification.

StandByDev also addresses another problem:

Developers can build good portfolios but still receive almost no attention.

A developer's portfolio and projects can become discoverable through the StandByDev ecosystem.

The platform can therefore become another source of visibility and traffic for developers rather than simply another place to store a portfolio.

5. Ranking and Leaderboards

Developers are ranked based on their reputation.

The platform has a leaderboard system where developers can see their position relative to other developers.

There can be different ranking contexts, including:

Global ranking
Skill/category-based ranking
Other future ranking systems

The important distinction is:

Score ≠ Rank ≠ Tier

Score

The numerical representation of a developer's reputation.

Rank

The developer's actual position on a leaderboard.

Tier

A classification based on leaderboard position.

The leaderboard is intended to make a developer's progress visible.

Instead of simply asking:

"How good am I?"

developers can see:

"Where do I currently stand?"

And because reputation changes, that standing can also change.

6. Progression

StandByDev is designed around continuous progression.

A developer doesn't simply create a profile once and finish.

Their standing can evolve as they:

Build more
Contribute more
Verify more evidence
Participate
Gain recognition
Compete
Work with clans
Complete activities
Become more active within the community

This creates an ongoing progression system around the developer's real work and participation.

7. Popularity

Reputation is not only about verification and technical evidence.

Popularity is another part of the ecosystem.

Developers can gain visibility and recognition from the community.

However, popularity is intended to be a separate dimension rather than simply allowing follower counts or popularity to completely determine developer reputation.

The broader idea is to combine:

what you build + what you contribute + what can be verified + how the community responds

into a more complete developer standing.

8. Clans

StandByDev introduces clans as a social and competitive layer.

Developers can create or join clans and participate as part of a group.

A clan can have:

Its own members
Its own community
Its own performance
Its own ranking
Group activities
Clan competitions

This creates a layer beyond individual developer competition.

Instead of only asking:

"Where do I stand?"

developers can also ask:

"Where does my clan stand?"

9. Clan Leaderboards

Clans can have their own leaderboard.

Clan performance can be compared against other clans.

This creates two connected ranking systems:

Individual standing

and

Clan standing

Individual developers can therefore build their own reputation while also contributing to the performance and reputation of their clan.

10. Clan Battles

Clans can compete against each other through clan battles.

These are larger group-based competitions where clans compete through different forms of developer activity and contribution.

Potential evaluation areas explored include:

Ideas
Problem solving
Popularity
Overall contribution to the competition

The purpose is to create meaningful group competition rather than simply putting developers on an individual leaderboard.

Clan battles are also intended to strengthen the social relationship between developers and give clans an ongoing reason to participate.

11. Activities and Challenges

StandByDev can include activities that developers participate in as part of their progression.

These can include:

Developer challenges
Coding activities
Mini-games
Creative challenges
Platform activities

The platform can also allow developers themselves to create challenges, rather than making all activities platform-created.

This allows the community to contribute to the culture and activities of StandByDev.

12. Community

StandByDev has a broader developer community layer.

There can be an open community where developers can:

Discuss topics
Share ideas
Participate in conversations
Discover other developers
Interact with the broader developer ecosystem

Clans can additionally have their own private community space.

This creates two levels:

Global developer community

and

Private clan community

13. Competition and Seasons

StandByDev is intended to have recurring competitive periods rather than being a completely static ranking website.

Seasons, events, and festivals can create temporary competitive environments where developers and clans participate in different activities.

This gives the platform an evolving environment where the experience can change over time.

14. Festival-Based Leaderboard Experiences

One of the more experimental parts of the platform is the idea of developer-created leaderboard themes.

During festivals or special events, developers can create visual themes for the leaderboard.

The community and platform evaluation can determine which themes are selected.

Selected themes can then become part of the actual StandByDev leaderboard experience during the event.

The creator receives recognition for the theme.

This turns the leaderboard itself into something developers can contribute to, rather than something completely controlled by the platform.

The planned countdown system can progressively reveal selected themes leading into the event.

The larger idea is:

Developers don't only participate inside the platform — they can also contribute to the platform itself.

15. Developer Contribution

This is an important part of the overall philosophy.

StandByDev is not only about consuming features.

Developers can contribute to the ecosystem through things such as:

Projects
Challenges
Community participation
Clan activities
Competition
Leaderboard themes
Other future community-created experiences

So the platform can gradually become shaped by its developer community.

16. Developer Discovery

StandByDev can also become a place where developers discover other developers.

A developer can potentially be discovered through:

Their reputation
Rank
Skills
Projects
Verified evidence
Contributions
Popularity
Clan participation
Community activity

This creates a discovery layer around developer work.

Instead of developers only searching for opportunities themselves, their work and standing can also become a way for other people to discover them.

17. Recruiter and Professional Discovery

A future part of StandByDev is professional developer discovery.

Recruiters and companies can eventually search for developers using meaningful signals such as:

Skills
Categories
Rank
Reputation
Verified evidence
Projects
Professional identity

The developer's actual profile and portfolio remain the source of their professional presentation.

The goal is to make developer discovery more connected to demonstrated work and contribution.

This is a future stage of the platform, rather than the entire purpose of StandByDev.

18. Why Developers Keep Coming Back

Traditional developer profiles are mostly static.

You create them, add information, and then there is little reason to return.

StandByDev is designed to be different because a developer's standing can continuously change.

There are multiple reasons for continued participation:

Your rank can change
Your reputation can grow
New projects can contribute to your profile
Your popularity can change
You can participate in activities
You can join or compete with a clan
Clan rankings can change
Clan battles can happen
New seasons and events can occur
You can create challenges
You can participate in community discussions
Your projects can gain visibility
You can discover other developers
You can contribute to the platform itself

The platform therefore becomes an ongoing developer environment, rather than a profile that gets created once and abandoned.

19. What StandByDev Is Ultimately Building

StandByDev is building a system where a developer's work, contribution, reputation, competition, and community presence come together into one continuously evolving standing.

The progression is essentially:

BUILD

You create projects and do real developer work.

↓

PROVE

Your work and professional evidence can be verified.

↓

EARN REPUTATION

Your contribution becomes part of your standing.

↓

RANK

Your standing becomes visible relative to other developers.

↓

COMPETE

You compete individually and with clans.

↓

CONTRIBUTE

You participate in challenges, communities, events, and platform culture.

↓

GROW

Your standing evolves over time.

↓

CONNECT

Other developers, communities, and eventually professional opportunities can discover you.

The long-term vision is for StandByDev to become an ecosystem where a developer's work, proof, reputation, ranking, competition, contribution, community, and discovery continuously reinforce each other.

In one sentence

StandByDev is a developer reputation and competition platform where the things you build and contribute become your standing — giving developers a persistent identity, visible reputation, ranking, competition, community, and opportunities for discovery.
